# Open ecosystem stewardship, IP and sustainable funding

Status: design and legal-readiness baseline  
Scope: Calorie Ecosystem, CalorieApp, CalorieVerse, CalorieStudio and future ecosystem extensions

This document records the intended operating model. It is not a licence grant,
trade-mark filing, tax ruling, regulatory approval, or substitute for Dutch/EU
legal advice.

## Core objective

The project should support two goals at the same time:

1. people must be able to keep building independent applications, worlds,
   extensions and creator experiences around the ecosystem; and
2. the official products need a sustainable revenue model that can compensate
   the people funding and building the project and, over time, fund an
   increasingly independent ecosystem treasury/stewardship layer.

Open participation does not require giving away the official brand, private
user data, production credentials, or every line of official product code.

## Product and ecosystem boundary

The current product family is:

- **Calorie Ecosystem** — shared protocols, compatible data/capability
  boundaries and the long-term ecosystem layer;
- **CalorieApp** — the official food/nutrition application;
- **CalorieVerse** — the persistent live metaverse/open world;
- **CalorieStudio** — a creator surface inside CalorieVerse and directly in
  the ecosystem.

Independent projects may build compatible experiences under their own
namespace and branding. Compatibility does not automatically make a project an
official Calorie product.

## Open-building path

The long-term target is to make the ecosystem interfaces genuinely reusable
without requiring permission from the operator for every independent project.

The preferred structure is:

- public, versioned protocols and schemas;
- stable extension points and capability contracts;
- a separately identified set of reference SDKs/examples suitable for a
  recognised open-source licence after provenance review;
- portable creator assets and user-controlled data where legally possible;
- no requirement to use official hosted infrastructure merely to remain
  protocol-compatible.

The **existing repository is not automatically relicensed** by this document.
Its current repository licence remains controlling. Before any existing file is
moved into an open component, maintainers must verify authorship, contributor
rights, third-party code, asset provenance and patent/licence compatibility.

For newly cleared ecosystem protocol/SDK components, Apache-2.0 is the current
licence candidate because it is permissive and includes an express contributor
patent grant. Activation requires a file-level/component-level licence manifest
and explicit rights-holder approval. Documentation/content may need a different
licence and must be reviewed separately.

## Trade marks and official identity

Open ecosystem code and protocols do not imply an unrestricted right to use the
official names, logos or trade dress in a way that suggests endorsement.

The intended long-term model is:

- independent implementations may describe truthful compatibility;
- official marks remain separately governed;
- a future independent stewardship body may receive ownership of, or a durable
  licence to administer, ecosystem marks if the owner deliberately approves
  that transfer/licence and the required registry recordals are completed;
- no ownership transfer is automatic merely because governance becomes more
  distributed.

A neutral trade-mark policy should eventually distinguish:
- nominative compatibility use;
- community/project naming;
- certification/conformance badges;
- official product branding;
- prohibited misleading or impersonating use.

## Creator copyright and user-generated content

Creator ownership should be the default for original CalorieStudio works.
The platform should obtain only the rights it needs through clear terms, for
example a non-exclusive licence to host, cache, transform for technical display,
moderate, and show the work in CalorieVerse. Any commercial sublicensing,
marketplace sale or broader promotional reuse should require separately stated
terms.

Before public user-generated-content hosting is enabled, production terms must
cover at least:

- contributor authority and third-party rights;
- prohibited uploads and infringement reporting;
- notice-and-action / takedown handling;
- moderation and appeal;
- licence scope, duration, termination and technical backup/cache survival;
- attribution and provenance where applicable;
- treatment of AI-assisted/generated material;
- minors and age-appropriate creator features.

## Sustainable commercial model

The ecosystem can remain open while official services are commercial.

Candidate revenue streams include:

- optional premium CalorieApp or CalorieStudio features;
- optional premium creator tools, storage, rendering and collaboration;
- managed hosting, API/data services and enterprise support;
- managed node/compute/storage services where lawful;
- optional cosmetic/world content that is not pay-to-win;
- marketplace or transaction service fees only after separate legal,
  consumer, tax and regulatory review;
- sponsorships, partnerships, grants and paid implementation/support work.

Core Gameverse/CalorieVerse entry, ordinary ecosystem exploration and ordinary
CalorieStudio browsing should not require a wallet, token purchase or node
participation. Premium features must remain additive rather than degrading the
free core.

## Founder/developer recovery and ecosystem funding

The commercial model explicitly recognises that the current builder/funder may
need to recover documented project costs and receive reasonable development and
operating compensation. That is compatible with a long-term decentralisation
goal when the rules are transparent.

The intended funding path is phased:

### Phase A — build and recovery

Revenue may primarily fund:
- hosting and operating costs;
- legal, security and compliance work;
- documented development costs;
- reasonable founder/developer compensation;
- reserves needed to keep official services operating.

### Phase B — shared growth

As recurring revenue becomes dependable, an explicit share can be allocated to
an ecosystem treasury while founder/developer compensation continues under
documented terms.

### Phase C — mature stewardship

The long-term goal is that a substantial and potentially primary share of
ecosystem-derived surplus funds ecosystem maintenance, public infrastructure,
grants, moderation/security, creator tooling and approved community development.
The founder/operator may still be paid for real services, licences or defined
roles, but should not need indefinite unilateral treasury control for the
ecosystem to function.

No fixed percentages are declared yet. Percentages, definitions of revenue vs
profit, cost-recovery caps, related-party payments, treasury custody and
governance authority require explicit recorded approval plus accounting/tax
review before activation.

## Separation of money and governance

Paying for premium services does not buy governance authority. Token ownership,
premium status, marketplace spend and node contribution do not automatically
grant control over official product state or private user data.

A future treasury or stewardship body should have:
- a written purpose and spending mandate;
- transparent revenue categories and related-party disclosures;
- auditable accounts;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- a documented transition/handover process;
- no ability to override privacy, security, legal or user-consent boundaries.

A Dutch stichting/foundation or another independent legal vehicle is one
possible future stewardship structure, but no entity choice is made by this
document. Corporate, tax, governance and liability advice is required before a
transfer.

## Paid digital services and EU launch gates

Before charging EU consumers for subscriptions, premium digital tools, virtual
items or marketplace services, the launch checklist must address:

- consumer information, conformity/remedy and cancellation rules for digital
  content/services;
- VAT place-of-supply, invoicing and OSS obligations where applicable;
- privacy/GDPR and data-minimisation;
- Digital Services Act duties if the service hosts/intermediates creator
  content or operates marketplace/platform functions;
- age and parental-consent design where minors are served;
- refund, chargeback and fraud handling;
- clear distinction between game/premium purchases and regulated financial or
  crypto-asset services.

Any CAL/XRP payment rail, token reward, exchange, custody, transfer execution or
other crypto-asset service remains behind the separate MiCA/regulatory review
boundary already recorded in the repository.

## Evidence and launch checklist

Before the public commercial launch of CalorieVerse/CalorieStudio:

1. perform an exact EUIPO/TMview/BOIP clearance search for the final public
   names and relevant goods/services;
2. do not claim CalorieVerse is registered unless a registration actually
   exists; do not use the registered symbol for an unregistered mark;
3. retain the official register extracts/certificates for existing registered
   marks;
4. complete asset and contributor provenance for all shipped visual/audio/3D
   content;
5. create the open-component licence manifest before claiming a component is
   open source;
6. publish production Terms, Privacy, creator/content rules and IP-reporting
   process;
7. document paid-product VAT/consumer-law handling;
8. perform the separate crypto/MiCA review before activating real-token rails;
9. record any future transfer/licence of marks or other IP to a stewardship
   entity with the appropriate signed instruments and registry recordals.

The engineering repository can prepare these boundaries, but filings,
assignments, tax positions and final consumer/legal terms require the relevant
rights holder and qualified advisers.
